Methods

What this is

Burn Calendar tracks detected fire activity within the Natural Earth California boundary, day by day since 2000. From 2012, a model estimates MODIS‑Aqua-like occupied 1 km grid cells from VIIRS detections. Before then, the calendar uses MODIS detections directly. Differences in coverage, observation time and sensor response limit comparisons across periods; a consistent scale across the whole record has not been established.

The calendar is rebuilt every morning by an automated job. It is an activity index: it does not measure acres burned, count distinct fires, or predict escape.

Definitions & units

  • Activity: predicted MODIS‑Aqua occupied 1 km equal-area grid cells per pass, summed across a day. The grid is cylindrical equal-area (x = R·longitude, y = R·sin(latitude)); cell shapes distort toward the poles only.
  • FRP: fire radiative power in megawatts, summed across the day's cells; a measure of energy release rate, not extent.
  • Reported days: days on which VIIRS S‑NPP produced science-quality (or near-real-time) detections. A blank dashed cell means no observations were reported for that day; it does not mean zero fire.
  • MODIS-native era: 2000–2011 uses Terra before Aqua became available in mid-2002, and Aqua thereafter. Terra and Aqua observe at different times, and their readings have not been calibrated to one another.
  • Date indicator: the existing model uses a 2023 cutoff intended to approximate a collection transition. The actual collection cannot be inferred reliably from date, and the observed ratio shift has not been shown to be caused by that transition. The MODIS product guide describes C6 and C6.1 active-fire products as sufficiently consistent to intermix.

Data sources

  • Before VIIRS, the calendar uses Terra or Aqua MODIS directly, preserving the original sensor choice for each day. These observations have not been adjusted for differences in overpass time or coverage.
  • NASA FIRMS area API: S-NPP standard-processing and near-real-time data through November 2, 2026, followed by NOAA-20. The API is queried with a bounding box and then detection centres are filtered to the Natural Earth California polygon, including islands and points on its boundary. The polygon is an approximate geographic boundary; satellite coordinates represent pixel centres.
  • The archive from 2000 through the latest published science month was bootstrapped from the CC0 Source Cooperative FIRMS mirror (NASA LANCE republished verbatim), cross-checked row-for-row against FIRMS API extracts of the same windows.
  • Historical extracts for 2012–2026 originally omitted a narrow coastal strip west of −124.4°. A targeted archive backfill found no reported detections there in that period. The live query extends to −124.5° before filtering to the state boundary.
  • Science-quality SP products trail real time by roughly 2–4 months; recent months are served from NRT and upgrade in place to SP as the science files are published. Days currently served from NRT are labeled as such in the day view.
  • Cleaning is conservative and identical across history: vegetation-type detections only (type = 0), VIIRS confidence nominal or high, 10-minute overpass windows, 1 km grid occupancy.

The model

The current Poisson model was fitted on 682 co-detected S‑NPP/Aqua overpasses from 2015–2026 over the broader California bounding box, not retrained on the polygon. It uses occupied cells, FRP summaries, spatial context, scan/track, time of day, season and a date indicator. Its coefficients are in pipeline/model/ca_model.json. The training cohort may exclude passes where MODIS saw no fire, and does not represent all observation conditions.

Experimental activity forecast

After a successful daily refresh, Timestral-5M estimates seven future days of the satellite-detected activity index. Purple cells are forecasts, not observations; they are excluded from the map, archive, totals and rankings. The forecast uses the previous 730 UTC days, treats unreported days as missing, and does not use weather or incident data. It does not forecast wildfire ignition, spread, risk, or emergency conditions.

In a retrospective test on reported days in 2023–2025, using the archive available on November 15, 2026, the sum of median estimates was about 73% below reported activity; the top 10% of reported values were especially underestimated. The median is not an expected total. The 10th–90th percentile range covered about 76% of reported test values, and has not been independently calibrated. Missing future observations were not scored, and the historical archive was not reconstructed as it was known at each forecast date. NOAA-20 transition accuracy is unknown. These estimates are not suitable for fire safety or operational planning. Timestral model documentation.

Updates

Every day at 07:23 UTC, a GitHub Actions job pulls SP data for the current year and at least the trailing 120 days, plus a trailing 120-day NRT window for the relevant sensor, then rebuilds the calendar and map together. Pass activity is allocated across 5 km map cells in proportion to their occupied 1 km cells. The site is fully static; all scoring happens ahead of time.

Fine print

  • From November 3, 2026 the refresh uses NOAA‑20, applying the S‑NPP-trained model without a validated NOAA bridge. These estimates are provisional and may not be directly comparable to earlier dates. NASA's VIIRS guide describes the sensor products, which are generated independently; it does not establish a correction for this California index.
  • The 2000–2011 segment is MODIS-native, but Terra and Aqua have different sampling times. Treat cross-era comparisons as approximate.
  • Blank cells are unreported, not zero. Detection geometry (scan edge, off-nadir view) and sensor response differences are why the harmonization model exists.

Code, pipeline, coefficients, and the open data file: github.com/yashneil75/burn-calendar. The underlying detections are NASA's public-domain FIRMS products.
